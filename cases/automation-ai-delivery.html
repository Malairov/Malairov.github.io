Automation and AI-Enabled Delivery With Existing Tools

I built a connected operating system with existing Microsoft 365 tools, then designed and implemented a local ordering application with AI coding assistants. Exact matches move automatically. Uncertain ones stay with a person.

  • RoleSole on-site lead
  • SettingProduction-critical program at a major aerospace manufacturer
  • ConstraintNo additional spend
  • MethodAutomation with review and audit controls
Existing Microsoft 365 toolsNo additional spendHuman review for uncertain matches
Problem

One lead, scattered inputs and manual reconciliation.

The work arrived through email, vendor platforms and spreadsheets. Each reconciliation depended on manual checking.

I needed a system that could carry routine work across shifts while keeping exceptions visible. I designed it around the tools already in place, with no additional spend.

Decision support

Test the ordering policy before anyone relies on it.

I designed and implemented a Python-based local web application with AI coding assistants for weekly ordering decisions.

The model uses Monte Carlo simulation with recency weighting, demand classification, service-level policy by item criticality and lead time measured from receipts. An earlier planning model covered 523 items with 4,000 scenarios per item.

Releases are gated by 55 automated tests. The application runs in a 6–8 week shadow-mode pilot against a recorded baseline before its recommendations are relied on.

Routine work moved; decisions stayed visible.

The workflows cover 48–66 hours a month of manual-equivalent work, based on my time estimates across 10 processes. Two of those processes were not feasible manually before.

Weekly order preparation went from about five hours to about 30 minutes. My recommendations on the automated model, request management and ordering were adopted by the program’s executive sponsor. Managers from other accounts asked for consultations on the approach.

Strategy

Stabilize, harden, migrate, scale.

The pattern is reusable: automated intake, a master log with statuses, reconciliation and exceptions, usage-based recommendations, then a measured pilot.

I would stabilize the current flows, harden their controls, migrate them when a supported platform is available and scale the pattern only after the pilot shows dependable results.
