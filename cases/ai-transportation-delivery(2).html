Case 02 · Business delivery & product scale-up · Jul 2022–Apr 2025

From Technical Project to Scalable Business Product

Directed the delivery system that moved a multidisciplinary AI transportation initiative from requirements and technical integration through field validation, productization, sponsor acceptance, and scale — resulting in six product configurations and deployment across approximately 400 locations.

01 / Business challenge

The hard part was not building features. It was turning specialist work into one controllable delivery system.

Computer vision, hardware, software, procurement, QA, installation, field conditions, and sponsor expectations all moved on different clocks. Business value depended on making them converge at the same decision points.

I structured the work around a single delivery model: requirements became baselines, baselines became integrated plans, risks and changes had owners, tests produced acceptance evidence, field learning fed controlled decisions, and approved outcomes moved into product configurations that could scale.

Delivery remitI owned the management system around the work — planning, dependencies, RAID, change impact, QA gates, sponsor reporting, pilot coordination, and handover. Engineering design remained with technical leads.
Without a delivery system

Technical progress could exist without business readiness.

  • Teams progressed at different speeds
  • Dependencies surfaced late
  • Field issues mixed product defects with site conditions
  • Sponsor decisions lacked one consolidated view
Business delivery model

Every phase ended in evidence, a decision, or a controlled handoff.

  • Integrated milestones and dependency ownership
  • Risk, issue, defect, and change separation
  • Acceptance criteria and readiness gates
  • Decision packages translated technical work into business choices
02 / Delivery operating model

Six processes moved the product from ambiguity to repeatable delivery.

The operating model connected business intent, technical execution, governance, and field evidence instead of treating project management as a reporting layer around engineering work.

01 · Frame

Requirements & baseline

Convert sponsor needs, constraints, interfaces, and acceptance expectations into a controlled delivery baseline.

Output: agreed scope, priorities, acceptance logic
02 · Integrate

Plan & dependencies

Sequence hardware, software, procurement, QA, installation, and sponsor dependencies against milestones.

Output: integrated schedule and ownership map
03 · Control

Risk, issues & change

Separate risks, defects, assumptions, dependencies, and changes; assign owners and quantify delivery impact.

Output: decision-ready RAID and change packages
04 · Validate

QA & acceptance gates

Connect requirements to test evidence, defect status, retest results, site readiness, and go/no-go conditions.

Output: evidence-based release decisions
05 · Learn

Pilot & field feedback

Classify live-site failures as product defects or site adaptations so the baseline stays clean and scalable.

Output: controlled corrective actions and lessons
06 · Scale

Productization & handover

Stabilize approved configurations, transfer decisions and evidence, and move from project activity to repeatable deployment.

Output: scalable product configurations and operating handoff
Management logic Business need → controlled baseline → coordinated execution → evidence → sponsor decision → product configuration → scale.
03 / Governance & decision rights

Meetings did not govern the project. Explicit decision rights did.

01

Integrated planning

Microsoft Project controlled the milestone-driven hardware baseline; Jira managed iterative software and monitoring work. Procurement, QA, installation, and sponsor dependencies remained visible across both.

02

Change impact

Changes were translated into schedule, cost, risk, technical, and acceptance impact before sponsor review instead of entering delivery as informal requests.

03

Acceptance gates

Requirements, test evidence, defects, retest status, site conditions, and exceptions formed a clear readiness state for release and pilot decisions.

04

Sponsor decisions

Weekly sponsor syncs reduced technical detail to the decisions that mattered: current state, delivery risk, impact, options, and next priority.

01

Signal

Risk, defect, dependency, or request is raised.

02

Assess

Impact on scope, schedule, cost, risk, and acceptance is consolidated.

03

Recommend

Options and delivery consequences are prepared for decision.

04

Decide

Sponsor-controlled governance approves, rejects, or redirects.

05

Control

Baseline, owners, actions, and evidence are updated and tracked to closure.

Decision authority remained with the sponsor board. My role was to make the decision complete, traceable, and executable.

04 / Field-to-business feedback loop

Field failures became structured business learning instead of uncontrolled rework.

Live deployment exposed conditions that lab testing could not fully reproduce. The management task was to classify what changed, assign the right owner, and protect the product baseline while still moving the pilot forward.

Detect

Capture the actual operating failure

Examples included night glare, wind-related camera movement, and winter survivability constraints.

Classify

Separate product defect from site adaptation

This prevented one local condition from silently redefining the product baseline.

Control

Assign owner, impact, action, and retest path

Technical work was linked to delivery risk, evidence, and acceptance rather than tracked as an isolated engineering task.

Learn

Feed validated learning into the next configuration

Approved adaptations and fixes became part of controlled product evolution and future deployment readiness.
